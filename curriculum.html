THE SUBJECTS, UP CLOSE

Different subjects.
Connected ideas.

A pattern can lead to an equation. A question about a leaf can lead to biology. These example topics show how the starting point can grow with the learner.

AREA OF STUDYBUILD A FOUNDATIONTAKE IT FURTHER

∑Math

Build a foundationNumber sense, shapes, fractions, and measurement.

Take it furtherRatios, algebra, geometry, and interpreting data.

Biology

Build a foundationLiving things, habitats, and the needs of plants and animals.

Take it furtherCells, ecosystems, genetics, and body systems.

Chemistry

Build a foundationMaterials, mixtures, and solids, liquids, and gases.

Take it furtherAtoms, chemical reactions, and the periodic table.

Physics

Build a foundationPushes and pulls, light, sound, and everyday motion.

Take it furtherForces, energy, waves, and electrical circuits.

Earth science

Build a foundationRocks, weather, seasons, and the water cycle.

Take it furtherEarth’s systems, climate, and changes across geologic time.

FROM QUESTION TO UNDERSTANDING

Make room for
the “why.”

A simple rhythm for exploring an idea together.

Talk to us about a school visit ↗
  1. 01

    Start with something familiar.

    A shape, a plant, a moving object. Connect the topic to something a student can notice or describe.

  2. 02

    Try an idea. Look closely.

    Work through a problem, draw a model, or compare observations. Leave room to change an explanation.

  3. 03

    Explain it in your own words.

    Share the reasoning, ask one more question, and connect the discovery to the next idea.
